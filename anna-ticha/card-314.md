# Card 314: a letter for tomorrow and concern for the recipient

[Anna Tichá investigation](README.md) · [All card readings](readings.md)

Research recorded on 9 October 2026. Preliminary picture-side reading by Codex (GPT-6), applying the existing published key without changes. The eleven-line block retains a dense unresolved cluster and several source sequences that require editorial changes to produce fluent Czech.

A separately tasked Codex reviewer (GPT-6-astra) compared all 55 source contexts and the written date, with the key and proposed text visible. ## Partial literal

Our proposed reading of [HCPortal record 1633, pc_hcp_314](https://crypto.hcportal.eu/dashboard/cryptograms/1633) is:

```text
vrely dik, milenko moje
predraha, za dnesni listek.
psanko jest jiz rozepsana a
zitra je dokoncim a odeslu.
do je ti, drahousku, ze poutana
jsi na luzko? jest do vaz nejsi
horoba? jsem velice znepokojen.
nesmis mi stonati. vzpominam
na te stale a touzim po tobe
nesmirne. tisi[U2]ero pozdravu
a pus od jedine tveho pepouse.
```

The ledger records 253 proposed mapped bodies in 55 contextual groups, using 21 existing classes. These all emit one letter. U2 is an overwritten cluster between `tisi` and `ero` in L10W2; neither its segmentation nor a body count is accepted. The mapped total is therefore not a complete block count or an accuracy measure. No secondary V2 transformation or invented overhead cap is applied.

## Meaning and editorial operations

The opening thanks “my dearest beloved” for today's note and says a letter is already being written, to be finished and sent tomorrow. The middle asks about the recipient being confined to bed and expresses strong worry: `jsem velice znepokojen`. The writer then asks her not to be ill, says he continually remembers her and longs for her greatly, and closes with greetings and kisses from “your Pepouš.” This is an interpretation of affectionate correspondence, not a diagnosis or independent evidence of an illness.

Several literal sequences must remain distinct from a fluent translation:

| Literal record | Possible editorial display | Required operation |
|---|---|---|
| `rozepsana` | `rozepsáno` | Replace accepted final `a` with `o`, plus accents; not source-confirmed. |
| `do je ti` | `co je ti` | Replace accepted `d` with `c`; the source has G06. |
| `jest do` | `jest to` | Replace accepted `d` with `t`; the source has G06, not the left-bowl t class. |
| `vaz nejsi` | `vážnější` | Join contextual groups and add accents; accepted base letters unchanged. |
| `horoba` | `choroba` | Change `h` to `ch`; no associated cap was established for the source's G10. |
| `tisi[U2]ero` | `tisícero` | Supply `c` from unresolved ink, plus accents; not accepted in the literal. |

These are proposals, not silently applied corrections. In particular, the exact illness question and the amount of greetings are not fully established. The two `d` assignments were initially guessed as `c` and `t` from context and corrected after source comparison. The proposal `vaz` was considered with the language visible; it is not an independent prediction. U2 remains unknown even though a familiar closing formula suggests a convenient letter.

The familiar-name interpretation of `pepouse` is consistent with other reports but does not identify a legal name or prove common authorship. The written date and the promised letter do not establish whether any letter was actually sent or received.
