## Main finding

The letter asks the addressee to send the number associated with the writer’s own likeness or portrait, which the writer says they want reproduced. The wording supports a planned reproduction, without establishing photography, a numbering system, the number itself, a copy quantity, a completed order or an identified person.

The text also gives New Year wishes, describes the writer’s unspecified illness and having to lie down, mentions a cousin separately, and ends with an affectionate future-oriented question. Some smooth readings require the explicit unknown completions or letter repairs recorded below. The first literal remains unchanged.

## Date, address and prior exposure

The ordinary inscription reads “31. / XII. 1900.”, or 31 December 1900. Fresh source readers agree, but the project had already inspected this card’s date and address during the earlier address audit. This is not a globally pristine date test. The address names “slečna Anna Tichá / učitelka v / Kamenici nad Lipou.” It supplies a mailing destination and occupational wording, not a school, employment record, residence or unique civil identity.

The printed greeting is “Srdečné blahopřání k novému roku.” (“Heartfelt congratulations for the New Year.”). Printed “No. 6515.” also appears. That printed number has not been linked to the likeness/portrait number requested in the cipher text. Neither is silently substituted for the other. The painted birds and flowers identify no real person or event.

Both original images were already in saved address-timeline evidence. This experiment used local byte-identical copies and checked them against the earlier retrieval hashes. No new source download occurred. Two other loci each permit one extra body. This shared-count constraint is preserved and independently audited.

The shallow/wavy third cap does not pass strict geometry. No primary UNKNOWN is eligible for V2; secondary output adds nothing and is byte-identical to primary. Source readers differ in how they group the page into regions. Local line order and source spacing are preserved; the displayed main/lower grouping does not prove a single global traversal. Punctuation was interleaved geometrically with the frozen visual segments, without lexical decisions.

## Conservative meaning

The central request “sdel mi laskave … cislo me podobizny, kterou chci dati rozmnoziti” is interpretable through accents and ordinary spacing as a request for the number of “my likeness/portrait,” which the writer wants reproduced. The relative pronoun refers to the portrait as the object of reproduction. The passage does not say what physical medium it uses, why it has a number, how many copies are desired, or that copying happened. It supplies no full identity or actual requested number.

The illness passage grammatically concerns the writer. It supports an unspecified illness and being obliged to lie down, with a probable separate reference to being at home. No disease, severity, medical treatment or exact illness location is established. The cousin appears in a different clause; nothing says the cousin or the addressee was ill. “This evening” is a plausible completion of an unknown, not a source-verified timing anchor.

Anduličko, a plausible affectionate name form, and the signature Pepouš do not identify civil persons or establish their relation beyond the letter’s affectionate language. The lower passage is probably a future question about kissing the addressee’s sweet lips in the new year; it needs disclosed letter repairs and does not establish an actual meeting, promise or fulfilled plan. No date of composition, dispatch or receipt is assigned from the ordinary inscription.

## Unresolved source loci

- L01_B006/B007: overwritten pair, jointly 2–3 bodies. Visible G06/G09-like bases are not accepted as complete forms. Natural greeting candidates include “nejdražší” and “nejbližší,” but language alone cannot select one or allocate the possible extra body.
- L05_B014: a low mark/body in “?sem.” “Jsem” is a plausible linguistic completion; the source remains unassigned.
- L05_B018: smudged one- or two-body span in “?oma.” “Doma” is plausible, without resolving body count or proving the illness location.
- L09_B004: arc/slash group in “dne?vecer,” with one- or two-body alternatives. “Dnes večer” is plausible but unaccepted as a source completion.

## Reproducibility and limits

A separate audit exactly reproduces the complete literal and validates 331 mapped-class re-encryptions under the unchanged table. Sixteen t positions need the observed allograph tag because the established inverse contains two graphical t forms. Twenty-two mapping entries occur. The five unknown loci are not claimed re-encrypted or verified.

The audit independently applies the joint-count constraints, confirms the shared overstrike extra is counted once, and checks that the only two strict-eligible marks belong to already-mapped G10_CAP units. A field name in the first replay script’s eligibility assertion was corrected after execution; the original script is preserved, the independent explicit eligibility audit confirms zero eligible unknowns, and no first literal, count or inference changed. The correction and original script hash are documented in the evidence.

These are consistency and coverage checks, not verified historical accuracy, an exact complete key or external expert authentication. Native crops preserve original image pixels and honest contextual coordinates. All transcription, graphical and linguistic reviews are AI-assisted. No external human expert validation is claimed.
