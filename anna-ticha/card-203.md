## Important date limit

The handwritten day and month read 12 January, but the written year is not securely resolved. Reader A froze “12./1. 1901.” with 7 as an alternative; reader B froze “12./1.1907.” A third independent date-only reader, given no earlier candidates or catalogue label, froze the conservative “12./1.190[?].” It tentatively favors a serifed 1 while retaining 7, and does not accept a complete year.

The saved catalogue labels this card 12 January 1904. That remains catalogue metadata, not a source-confirmed inscription date. The disagreement does not prove that the catalogue date is wrong. Neither 1901 nor 1907 is established. Do not use this card to narrow an address-date interval or to fill the previously noted 1905–1907 metadata gap.

The third reader finds a tall slanting stem and broad baseline foot resembling the secure 1 elsewhere in the same date, but an additional wavy upper-middle stroke permits an elaborately written 7. The available source pixels do not settle the intended numeral. The date’s function as composition, dispatch or receipt has not been established.

The address reads “Velectěná Slečna / slečna Anna Tichá / učitelka v / Žirovnici / u Počátek.” It supplies an address destination and occupational wording, not a school, residence, full civil identity or personal move. The pictured landscape is not evidence of where the writer or recipient was.

## Main finding

The letter discusses the timing of replies, affection, a difficult proposed meeting and a possible visit to Prague. The writer says the meeting on the indicated day will probably not be possible because he can scarcely leave the office, and suggests postponing it. He asks whether Anička would come to Prague as she had promised, and closes as “pepous”. A final clause concerns to whom she promised to go to a ball. These are conservative interpretations of the frozen text, with repairs and unknowns separated below; no journey, meeting, promise or attendance is independently established.

## Interpretation kept separate

The first region thanks Anička for a letter/card and disputes her complaint about how late the writer replied, comparing the time each took after receiving the other’s note. The affectionate tone is clear; her original message is unavailable, so the writer’s account does not independently establish her words or feelings.

The next regions express a desire to embrace and kiss her, discuss a meeting unlikely to be possible on the designated day, and explain difficulty leaving the office. R2→R3 is a strong linguistic continuation hypothesis, not a source-proven global traversal. “Úřad” does not identify an employer, institution, exact occupation or city of employment.

The Prague proposal and reference to a promised visit do not show that the promise was made or fulfilled. The ball clause concerns to whom an alleged promise was made, not necessarily a person who would accompany her. The affectionate signature plausibly reads Pepouš; it is not a full identity or proof of physical authorship.

## Unresolved records and editorial limits

- A203_0274a, R2 line 11: the left crossed component may contain zero or one independent body. The phrase “ano sko ml ?e” remains unclear. No definite vocative is reconstructed and no missing letter is assumed.
- A203_0303, R3 line 14: a tall hook does not securely match a complete keyed class. “poneva?z” strongly suggests “poněvadž” linguistically, but the proposed d remains an unknown-slot hypothesis.
- A203_0471, R4 line 22: the raised hook with a detached lower dot may be punctuation or a cipher body. The literal’s final ? denotes this unresolved record. It is not certified question-mark punctuation.

The resolved “za” in the final clause must change a→e to yield the natural “že”. That is a separate conjectural letter substitution, not licensed by the terminal mark. The resolved “nezhli” can be accented as “nežhli”; modernizing it to “nežli” would delete the h and must not be described as accent restoration alone. Historical/nonstandard spelling may account for some irregularities; this work does not identify their causes.

The full independent Czech review supplies English/Russian summaries and separate ledgers for accent-only changes, spacing, resolved-letter edits and unknown hypotheses. None is inserted into the frozen literal. The language reviewer saw only that literal and a stripped unknown-count packet, without the images, key files, dates, prior cards or earlier interpretations.
