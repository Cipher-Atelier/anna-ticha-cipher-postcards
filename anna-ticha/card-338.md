## Editorial interpretation

The readable phrases describe sending a picture of Lanškroun from an excursion and arriving on foot. The first occurrence, lanskrouna, supports the place-name reading with editorial capitalization and diacritics; the later lan??rouna remains partly unresolved. The address-side source separately names Anna Tichá, describes her as a teacher, and gives Pelhřimov as the destination.

The message describes a first pond on the left, dlouhy?, and a second nearer the town, kratky. These suggest “Long” and “Short,” but the final unknown after dlouhy remains unresolved and the literal alone does not establish formal pond names. The writer says he has swum across both ponds countless times.

The adjoining passage plausibly describes a large yellow building as a gymnasium, an academic secondary school, and recalls time spent there. The duration cannot be read, and the school and the writer’s connection to it remain unidentified.

A later phrase refers to more about the excursion in a contemporaneous note, if the proposed completion of ??ce as více is correct. The closing gives many greetings from p. That final letter is preserved without expansion into a name or title.

Modern municipal accounts name Dlouhý and Krátký in a pond chain northwest of Lanškroun and place Krátký beside the built-up area. This supports the proposed pond names and relative positions, although it does not establish their appearance in 1908. The town’s school history confirms that a gymnasium existed before 1908, but does not identify the building in this passage or the writer’s connection to it.

A separate AI reviewer given only the literal found connected Czech and recorded the unresolved passages; its review does not measure historical plaintext accuracy.

Reading ?ymnasium as gymnasium supplies an unknown initial g. Modern Czech gymnázium would also change the resolved s to z; it is a modernization, not the saved spelling.

The municipal gymnasium history gives today’s address as nám. J. M. Marků 113, records its opening in temporary rooms in 1872, and notes that the building hosted the emperor in 1894. These details were consulted after decoding.
