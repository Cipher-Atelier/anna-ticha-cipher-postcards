## Result and corrected interpretive limit

The preferred reading gives an affectionate thank-you for a letter and a promise to answer. A following passage expresses anticipation, but “spring,” “that,” and the object of “bring” require conjectural missing letters. In particular, mi ?e privede could include tě (“you”) or je (“them”). The addressee is not established as the object. A hoped-for visit or meeting must therefore remain a conditional interpretation, not the result itself. No completed visit, agreed arrangement, travel date, place or historical identity is established.

On the source-preferred 134-body partition, 130 positions are mapped and four remain UNKNOWN. One additional residual is unprinted only under the preferred zero-body choice; its optional one-body UNKNOWN remains in the evidence. Four accepted positions change under retained split/join alternatives, leaving 126 accepted assignments invariant across the recorded partitions. The conservative total is 133–137 bodies. The preferred fraction 130/134 (97.01%) applies only to that partition; it is incorrect to divide 130 by the entire 133–137 envelope as though the numerator were invariant. None of these figures is an accuracy score.

## Cautious English rendering

Warm thanks for the dear letter, my Andulka. I will answer it as soon as possible. I now look forward all the more to [spring?], since I can hope [that?] it will bring [you? / them?] to me. Warm greetings and a thousand kisses from [pe?ouse: unresolved signing form].

## Interpretive qualifications

These are sense renderings of one preferred-partition interpretation, with editorial sentence punctuation. “As soon as possible” depends on the preferred n in nejdrive; the na construction and “hope” in doufati are also partition-sensitive. Interpreting spring as the subject that will “bring” someone or something depends on reconstructing ja?o as jaro. The unspecified object need not be the addressee. The signing form pe?ouse remains unresolved, with no missing consonant selected and no legal-name expansion. Andulko is a familiar address-form, not an identity determination. “A thousand kisses” is affectionate language, not an event count or proof of relationship status.
