## S01

**Source-supported correction of a mapped glyph:** L01 C14, `noje` → `moje`. Target `[500,1128,554,1180]` has a low single shoulder, unlike the multi-hump n controls at L01 C09 in `anisko` (`[332,1125,391,1180]`) and C29 in `milovana` (`[925,1125,985,1180]`). The mapped m at C23 in `milovana` (`[754,1125,805,1180]`) supplies the positive comparison under existing G15=m / G14=n definitions.

The second candidate-aware reader corroborated one rounded shoulder, a right descending stroke and short rising exit, with no extra n-like shoulder. The next glyph is separate and no detached target mark was seen; nearby printed cords remain part of the context. This supports the specific local correction under the inherited mapping, not independent whole-word or key truth. The [revised source-supported proposal](../../research-updates/evidence/reader-edition-2026-10-20/card250/card250_source_supported_reading.txt) changes only this n → m; it does not replace the original literal.

## U01

**Unresolved full symbol:** L06 C10, `m?ti`. The base at `[335,1486,369,1555]` is i-like/G07, compared with same-word final i at C12 (`[391,1492,420,1555]`) and same-card clear i at L01 C24 (`[790,1125,835,1180]`). Irregular lower-left/downward ink, preceding-glyph crowding and unresolved mark ownership prevent accepting the whole symbol or accent, as the second reader also noted. **Strict `m?ti` remains; no i or í is newly accepted.** `‹míti?›` is contextual only.

*Míti* is the older/bookish infinitive *mít*. `Budeš mne míti … ráda` concerns continued affection, naturally translated as “love me”; it is not *máti* (“mother”).

## Names, normalization and remaining limits

`anisko` is retained as `Anisko`, the ciphertext form of address. It is not a postal-address-side observation. `Aniško?` is only an optional accent interpretation; the source's s-family does not establish c, so `Aničko` is not substituted and no historical identity follows.

Joining `vzpo / ?inajiciho` and `?a k`, plus ordinary accents, capitalization, commas and question punctuation, is editorial. A visible final question mark does not make the entire punctuation system diplomatic. No fresh claim is made about testing or presence of the old 24th unbarred-t assignment.

The inherited key remains conditional: the later seven-claim audit supplies no independent letter foundation for those selected claims, without disproving every mapping or passage. Card 204's reported external check does not validate card 250. No solver, scorer, key search or historical experiment was run for this edition.
