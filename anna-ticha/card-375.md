## Source date limit

Both fresh readers read 19/VIII and a year beginning 190, without a secure final year digit. The cautious source label is therefore 19 August, year 190?. The saved catalogue gives 19 July 1904; that is separate metadata and is not substituted for the inscription. Earlier source review already noted a possible VIII month and uncertain final year digit. This test does not provide a complete new chronological anchor or evidence of a letter from 1905–1907. The date’s function as composition, dispatch or receipt remains unassigned.

The ordinary address names Anna Tichá, “učitelka v / Žirovnici / u Počátek.” It is address wording, not proof of a school, employment record, residence, unique civil identity or personal move.

## Main finding

This long letter adds business/building concerns and concern for an ill woman to its affectionate language. It mentions a neighbor’s work at a shared wall and resistance to rebuilding a rear part of “our house.” It also expresses concern that a note might alert a woman to the seriousness of her illness and distress her. A newspaper passage refers to “pana buriana” and “narodnich listech,” with wording that could be sincere praise or irony. The act is unspecified and the person is not identified.

The place-like string “usti” plausibly reads Ústí, but no particular city is established. References to “our house,” contemplated travel and a wish to sell everything there do not establish legal ownership, completed building work, a sale or an actual journey. The woman’s name-like strings “karlusi” and “zarluci” remain different in the literal; harmonizing them requires two resolved-letter changes. No diagnosis or full identity is inferred.

## Interpretation by region

UPPER concerns business matters calling the writer to “usti,” a neighbor intending to build by a shared wall, resistance to rebuilding part of “our house,” and reported commencement of building work. Some connective wording and references to plaster require unknown completions and letter repairs. The damaged “sestre?yce” could refer to a female cousin or cousins, but neither that relationship nor number is secure. It does not establish that a wall is being removed; the likely repaired reference is to plaster.

RIGHT_WRAP says that “usti” does not attract the writer and expresses a preference to sell everything there and never return. This is a wish or hypothetical course, not a completed transaction or proof of holdings. It then mentions a report of serious illness in a clause ending “zarmou-”; joining it to LOWER’s “tila” is linguistically natural but remains a cross-region hypothesis.

LOWER expresses sympathy and concern about whether a note might distress the woman. “Painful” and “dangerous” require respectively a known-letter repair and an unknown completion. Neither establishes a diagnosis. The text does not say the proposed note was sent or received. The different name-like strings are not silently made identical.

The Burian/newspaper passage identifies no deed. Surface-positive wording about professional artists’ character and inner depth can be sincere or ironic; this test does not choose. “Národní listy” is a plausible title reading, without a verified article identification. Crucially, the literal is “vaera”: “yesterday” requires a→c repair to včera. Any later newspaper comparison must therefore treat both the date and this relative-time word cautiously. No calendar year is recovered from that reference here.

The close has an affectionate, yearning tone. A repaired “I would smother you with warm, hot kisses” is figurative in context, not evidence of literal violence or particular past encounters. The tiny continuation naturally suggests “Škoda, že nejsi zde” (“It is a pity you are not here”), but this uses five unknown records and is not accepted as decoded text. The margin closes with hyperbolic kisses and Pepouš, which remains a nickname/signature rather than a full identity.

## Selected letter-level repairs, all unapplied

The full bilingual review logs fifteen candidate repair entries separately from accent-only changes, spacing and unknown fills. Examples include:
- disicce → dusicce, potentially dušičce
- jem → jen
- stavoti → staveti or staviti; the vowel remains unsettled
- nac → nas and donuditi → donutiti
- sestre?yce → sestre?ice, still requiring an unknown fill before a cousin reading
- ozma?i?? → ozna?i??, still requiring unresolved completions
- omytka → omitka
- saolacne → spolecne, requiring two substitutions
- slacny → slecny
- polest → bolest, requiring a local continuation for bolestná
- zarluci → karlusi would require both z→k and c→s; this is not adopted as a name repair
- vaera → vcera
- pusumi → pusami
- bus → pus

None changes the first literal. The causes of irregularities—writing, classification or other unresolved features—are not established. Global region joins, place and name expansions, date assumptions and newspaper identification are separate questions.
