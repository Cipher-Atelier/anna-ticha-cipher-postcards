## Result

The preferred literal supports an affectionate thank-you and longing note addressed as Aničko and signed in the voice of Pepouš. It mentions a departure, asks how the recipient is doing in a place written as “budejo vicich,” asks how long “my little heart” will stay “v hradci,” sends passionate kisses and hopes for another message soon. The optional Czech display requires no change to a mapped base letter, but two unknowns and medium-confidence source assignments remain. This is not a complete or independently authenticated decipherment.

The plural-or-polite forms vašeho/vám coexist with intimate singular ti/tvůj. Companions, a second addressee, formal address or a particular relationship cannot be selected from that mixture. The departure clause permits more than one syntactic attachment. The text does not establish a route, exact travel date, meeting, full historical identity or formal relationship status. The ordinary handwritten date is preferably 14./VIII.1903.; the month is less secure than the day and year.

## Cautious English meaning

Aničko, my only one, a thousand thanks for the dear note. The writer recalls a departure or thinks throughout its day, wonders [unresolved ?ak, possibly “how”] the recipient is getting on in Budějovice, and expresses continuing remembrance and strong longing. The words ask how long “my little heart” will stay in Hradec, although the final mark is unresolved. Pepouš, “forever yours,” sends a thousand [unresolved ?louhých, possibly “long”], passionate kisses and looks forward to another message soon from his “dear little soul.”

“Po celý den vašeho odjezdu” can attach as “throughout the day of your departure,” or as “all day I recalled your departure” under an older/literary genitive construction. It must not silently become “ever since you left.” The objects of “I keep remembering” and “I miss” are implicit; a beloved is a contextual reading. The text’s order does not prove a Budějovice-to-Hradec itinerary.

Budějovice and Hradec are unexpanded lexical readings. Adding České, Králové, Jindřichův or another qualifier to the ciphertext would add content. The independently read ordinary address below supplies separate geographical context; any identification connecting it to the cipher’s Hradec remains an inference. The intimate diminutives establish neither age nor legal names. Masculine grammatical voice is not verified authorship or a real-world sex/gender finding.
