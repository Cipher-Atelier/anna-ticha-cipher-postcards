## Meaning and retained irregularities

The main passage addresses “my dearest Andulička,” thanks her warmly for a dear note, and refers to her written lines. The travel statement says the writer had to go home again on Sunday and arrived on Monday. The closing says “your Pepouš” continually remembers her and kisses her in spirit countless times. The card supplies no destination for the Monday arrival, reason for the journey or independently verified itinerary.

Source proposals need particular care:

- `lystek` and `nedely` retain G05 = `y`, whose plain one-shaped form differs from the cornered G07 = `i`. Modern `lístek` and `neděli` require replacing accepted `y` with `i`, as well as adding accents. The note and Sunday interpretations use those editorial spellings.
- `desim se ... na tvoje radky` is retained with G06 = `d`. Reading this as “I look forward indescribably to your lines” requires changing the first `d` to `t`, yielding `tesim`. That is a proposed editorial repair, not an accepted source letter.
- `vzpomina` ends in accepted `a`. It can be read as third-person `vzpomíná`, with the later `tvuj pepous` as its subject. No final `m` is added to turn it into first-person “I remember.”

The left inscription is compatible, with accents added, with “mému andílkovi, zlaté mé Andulce” — “to my little angel, my golden Andulka.” Its words occupy an oblique arrangement; the local rows above preserve their placement without proving a single traversal through both regions. The underlines beneath `memu` and `stale` and the separate oblique stroke beside the left inscription receive no key values. The separate stroke's relationship to the final dot is unresolved; the saved dot does not certify an exact full punctuation reading.

The familiar names are forms of affectionate address and a signing form. They do not identify civil persons or establish common authorship with another card. The verbal account does not independently prove that the journey took place.
