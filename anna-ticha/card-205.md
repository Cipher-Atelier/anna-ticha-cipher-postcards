## Main finding

The new literal discusses postal delay and disappointment over an expected card. A separate block says the writer spent time in the dining room where he normally eats, after leaving the office only after nine. Block A ends with “stedry vecer” (Christmas Eve); reading A directly into B would connect the dining-room account to Christmas Eve, but that cross-block traversal remains a linguistic hypothesis, not an independently established graphic sequence.

Every one of the 389 indexed cipher bodies receives an output under the unchanged key. This is 100% mapping coverage, not 100% historical accuracy or a verified error-free reading. Several malformed strings, source alternatives and two spacing uncertainties remain. The interpretation below does not silently repair them.

## Frozen primary literal

Visual segmentation, punctuation, line breaks and literal irregularities are retained. No accents or letter repairs have been supplied. Blocks are displayed in inventory order, not as a source-proven continuous reading.

```text
REGION A
vrely dik za drahy listek. divim se, ze muj
listek dosel teprve v patek, daval jsem
jej sam u nas do prislusne prihradky,
takze z prahy odesel ve stredu vecer.
tesil jsem se, za anicka ma nme vkpomene
z pelhrinova listkem a kdyz ani vcerb
neprisel, byl jsem smuten. stedry vecer

REGION B
stravil jsem v jidelne,
kde se stravuji. dostal
jsem se, jak jsem predvidal teprve
pozde po devate hodine z uradu,
tisic dlouhyh, vasnivy ch pus posila
ti vecne tvuj pepous.

REGION C
svemu srdecku z cele duce preje pepous.
```

## Conservative interpretation

Region A thanks the addressee for her card/note. The writer is surprised that his own note arrived only on Friday, says he personally put it in the appropriate compartment, and says it left Prague on Wednesday evening. These are his assertions; neither actual posting nor delivery is independently verified and the weekdays are not assigned calendar dates here.

The following clause is badly formed at the literal letter level: “za anicka ma nme vkpomene / z pelhrinova … vcerb”. A plausible reconstructed sense is that he expected Anička to remember him with a note from a place possibly meant as Pelhřimov, and was sad when it had not arrived. That interpretation requires multiple edits; the place, “yesterday” and exact wording are not securely literal. Pelhřimov is not promoted to a new verified address, residence, journey or identity fact.

Region B separately says “stravil jsem v jidelne, / kde se stravuji” and describes getting away from the office late after nine, as anticipated. It ends with affectionate kisses and “ti vecne tvuj pepous.” It gives no information about whether the writer was alone or with company, no exact employer or occupation, and no independent proof of the events. If A→B is the correct continuation, A’s Christmas-Eve phrase supplies what was spent there; the source test did not settle that traversal.

Region C contains a wish addressed to “svemu srdecku” and signed Pepouš. Its phrase “z cele duce” requires a consonant repair to become the familiar “z celé duše.” The printed New Year greeting is preserved as separate source context, without silently inserting its words into the cipher. Pepouš is an affectionate signature, not a full civil identification or proof of physical authorship.

## Eight conjectural letter-level repairs

- za → ze, interpreted with an accent as “že”
- ma → na
- nme → mne, an adjacent transposition
- vkpomene → vzpomene
- pelhrinova → pelhrimova, potentially “Pelhřimova”; the place remains conjectural
- vcerb → vcera, potentially “včera”
- dlouhyh → dlouhych, inserting c
- duce → duse, potentially “duše”; c→s is a consonant change, not merely an accent

The separate “vasnivy ch” → “vášnivých” proposal changes spacing and accents, not the base-letter sequence. The full review itemizes accent-only changes and all other operations separately. None has been applied to the first literal. Irregularities could arise from source writing, graphical classification or another unresolved cause; the current test does not decide which.

## Validation and limits

A separate computational audit exactly reproduced the complete literal and checked all 389 mapped classes against the unchanged key and inverse candidates. Eighteen t positions need the original allograph tag because the established table contains two graphical t forms. Twenty-two mapping entries occur. All mapped classes re-encrypt exactly with those tags. Primary and secondary outputs agree byte-for-byte.

These are reproducibility and computational-consistency checks. They do not verify every source classification, linguistic word boundary, historical statement, complete key or intended reading. Full mapping coverage does not remove the seven moderate-confidence assignments or their retained alternatives. The source and language reviews are AI-assisted, not external human expert validation. The language reviewer saw only the frozen literal and a stripped limitations packet, without source images, key, dates, earlier cards or prior interpretations.
