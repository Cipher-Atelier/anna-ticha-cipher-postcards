## Meaning and editorial limits

The opening gives repeated thanks for a very dear gift. A following clause mentions a `podobenka`, a likeness or small portrait, and a warm letter. With the explicit repairs and U1 fill below, a possible sense is that these gave the writer a very happy day. The medium, subject and delivery of the likeness are not independently established; no particular photograph or object on another card is identified.

The writer promises to reply as soon as possible and asks for a message when the recipient is already `v kamenici`. Reading this as a place name gives “in Kamenice,” but does not identify a particular locality or address, or prove that the recipient arrived. The following question contains `pre[U2]tucha`. Filling U2 with `d` gives `předtucha`, “premonition” or “foreboding”; the content of that concern is not supplied by this card, and the missing class is not accepted from language alone.

The right strip says the writer remains close to the recipient in spirit and contains `preje ti k novemu ro[k/z]u mnoho zdaru`, possibly a wish for success “for the new year” if A1 is read as `k`. Its upper loop is visible, but the distinguishing lower contour enters U3 on the neighboring row; no invariant class is accepted. The visible written date is 11 September 1900. The occasion and intended sense of “new year” are not established; neither a calendar New Year's celebration nor a birthday is inferred. The closing refers to kisses and gives the familiar signing form `pepous`, interpretable with capitalization and accents as Pepouš. This does not establish a legal name or common authorship with another card.
