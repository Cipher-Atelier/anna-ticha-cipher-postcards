## Task and method

Apply the existing [24-entry partial key and mark rules](key-and-rules.md) to a new source record without refitting assignments. Card 201 was selected as the lowest unexamined corpus number after recorded exclusions, before its images were inspected in this test.

Two separate AI readers made glyph-only transcriptions. A separate source-only adjudication was frozen before the key was applied. The source readers received no key values, candidate words or historical narrative. No new key assignment or language-driven source correction was introduced.

Both readers recorded 20 rows, 570 body-observation slots and 29 punctuation observations. Two compact spans allow one or two bodies, so the total remains **570–572 bodies**. The frozen consensus maps **567 slots** and preserves **three unresolved body/span records**. Coverage is approximately **99.1–99.5%**, not a plaintext-accuracy measurement.

Two accepted source classifications remain medium-confidence: L12.12 (G15) and L12.25 (G04). Seven capped short-shoulder forms were assigned on graphical grounds to the existing broad G10_CAP family. Four have strict V caps and three have bent/wavy caps; the latter do not qualify for strict V2 normalization. Strict V2 contributes no additional position, so primary and secondary outputs are identical.

## Interpretation, separate from the literal

The text is a coherent affectionate Czech letter. The speaker reassures Anička that he is not planning to marry, expresses affection, mentions a builders' ball on Saturday that he probably will not attend, and anticipates a decision on or for 1 February about whether “we” receive notice to leave a dwelling.

Five conjectural repairs would give more ordinary Czech: domnemkami → domnenkami, sana → sama, nevostu → nevestu, amiz → aniz, and mebude → nebude. None is inserted into the literal or accepted as a source correction.

The three unknown records remain:

- **P345, line 10:** one or two bodies. The linguistic possibilities u or ua would support “láskou” or “láskou a”; neither has been accepted from the source.
- **P370, line 11:** a G03-like body and an elevated stroke with unresolved ownership. A possible e would give “ve mně,” but the stroke may be separate punctuation.
- **P443, line 13:** one or two bodies. Possible t or ti would give “jít” or “jíti”; segmentation remains unresolved.

Literal pepous plausibly represents **Pepouš**, an affectionate name or signature. It does not uniquely identify the sender or physical writer. The spacing dostane me is interpreted as dostaneme, **“we,” not “I.”** The other people covered by that plural remain unknown.

The separate ordinary inscription reads 19./I.1904. The address identifies Anna Tichá as a teacher in Žirovnice near Počátky. The inscription, postal markings, mailing destination and decoded narrative remain distinct evidence. They do not establish a school, residence, actual eviction, attendance at the ball or the identity of the pictured woman.

## Consistency checks

The saved literal reproduces exactly from the frozen ledger. All 567 mapped source classes re-encode exactly under the unchanged table when their observed t-allograph tags are retained. The inverse is non-unique at 35 t positions. The three unknown spans are excluded from the re-encoding claim.

A separately written audit checked the ledger, lookup, inverse candidates, output rendering, unknown counts and identical primary/secondary outputs. These are computational consistency checks. They do not establish every handwriting classification, unique segmentation, spacing, historical intention or a complete cipher model.

All graphical, transcription and language reviews were **AI-assisted**, not external human expert validation.
