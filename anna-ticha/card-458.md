## Editorial interpretation

The clearer passages thank the addressee for a note/card, promise to write soon, and ask when she will travel for the holidays and how long she will stay in Pelhřimov. The stated purpose is to know where to send a letter. The text does not identify which holidays are intended.

The phrase about the meaning of l?kov / ce has the possible botanical reading lýkovce, “daphne,” but that word remains a conjecture. It does not establish a flower’s symbolism or an exchange of a particular plant.

The lower region resembles an affectionate closing signed pepouš. It may continue the preceding reference to “your sweet” lips, but the spatial listing does not establish that reading order. The unresolved vin? admits first- or third-person interpretations.

The postcard’s printed caption is Máchův „Máj“ 2. Its verse reproduces the opening of Karel Hynek Mácha’s Máj, a love-themed Czech poem. The number 2 belongs to the postcard caption; the particular book edition used is unestablished. The reason for using a cipher remains unknown. This printed-context identification and date check were made separately after the cipher test; they are not additional cipher validation.

## Uncertainties

Joining bo and pis produces bopis. The proposed reading dopis, “letter,” changes an already resolved b to d; that repair is not made in either literal.

The source region sequence is a spatial listing. The bottom line may contain separate continuations, so connecting it to the right block is conditional.

A separate post-test image-only review read the bottom inscription as “4. IV. 1900.” No posting or receipt date is established.
