Postcard 198: English reading edition
Date inscribed on the postcard: 30 January 1904
Editorial version: 20 October 2026

Words in square brackets with a question mark are contextual restorations or proposed repairs, not newly established readings of the cipher signs. The translation follows the Czech reading edition. Its unbracketed passages reflect the previously published working reading, not an independently authenticated historical plaintext.

Heartfelt thanks, darling, for [today’s?] dear note. I had hoped that I would be able to go to [Budějovice?] after all, but it simply could not be managed. Still, I look forward to our meeting during the spring and to being able to kiss my one and only sweetheart passionately.

In Sviny, [remember?] me too. [Kindly?] pass on my regards to Miss Janková.

My sweetheart is probably [getting ready?] for the ball just now. What a pity that I [cannot?] come to you now. A thousand [long?], passionate kisses from Pepouš, who thinks of you with longing.

Editorial notes
1. Six bracketed word restorations correspond to seven unresolved source positions: [today’s?] to 20–21; [Budějovice?] to 79; [remember?] to 184; [Kindly?] to 205; [getting ready?] to 252; and [cannot?] to 278. All seven positions remain unresolved in the preserved literal transcription. The joined region at 20–21 also retains a physical segmentation uncertainty; the proposed word does not establish the number of signs.
2. [Long?] is a separate editorial repair, not another unresolved source position. The saved literal is dlohych. The expected Czech dlouhých would require inserting an unattested u. A writing omission is possible but has not been established.
3. Sviny is a working interpretation of the place-name in ve svinech; no particular municipality is identified here. Janková and Pepouš are readings of a name and an affectionate nickname, not established historical identities. Budějovice remains bracketed because its final source sign is unresolved.
4. The English renders srdéčko, literally “little heart,” as “sweetheart,” and mé poručení as “my regards.” In the closing, “you” makes explicit the naturally implied object of the writer’s recollection; it does not represent an additional cipher word. The translation does not attempt to reproduce the Czech historical infinitives odejeti and zlíbati or the literal construction do plesu.
5. This is a new reading edition and translation of an already published partial decipherment. It supplies no newly source-secure letters. Czech diacritics, capitalization, punctuation and the joining of divided words are editorial; the original literal and the separate uncertainty record remain unchanged.
