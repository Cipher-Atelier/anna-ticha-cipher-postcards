A small isolated dot follows p4 and a single dot plus dash follows q, rather than a secure colon. The final separate dot is retained. - Row 4: Both short disputed bodies intersect postmark ink. The possible caron above the third body is not securely separable from the stamp; the final downstroke after f5 could be 7, r, marked r or an unassigned form. Select two unknown slots, not CH or J, and retain the first reader's visible spacing as provisional. Initial open-hook g, following 6, 3 and f5 retain readable contours; possible initial q and 5/3 alternatives remain historical.
- Row 6: The second body has the horizontal top and descending curve of 5. The last body of m5cn4hm6 has a lower closed oval and ascending hook like 6, unlike the flat top of 5. Select the second reader's two digit-family choices on these contours. Stamp fragments still limit certainty and the first choices 6/5 remain recorded.

Retain the actual terminal question hook and dot. The intervening horizontal stroke is recorded as a short hyphen; dash length remains an alternative.
- Row 8: In phg3 the third body has an open hooked lower tail matching g, rather than a closed q return. Replace that obscured cluster with one unknown slot, retaining both full d-based readings and the first c?-family alternative. The following returning q and 5n are separately selected.
